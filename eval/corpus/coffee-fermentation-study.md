# Research Summary: Anaerobic Coffee Fermentation

This summary describes a small trial run with three farms in the highlands. The goal was to see
whether anaerobic fermentation improves cup scores without raising the share of defective
beans.

## Method

Freshly picked cherries were sealed in food-grade tanks without oxygen for 72 hours at a
temperature between 18 and 22 degrees Celsius. A control group from the same harvest was
processed with the farms' usual washed method. Each lot was dried on raised beds to a moisture
content of eleven percent.

## Results

The anaerobic lots scored on average 2.1 points higher on the 100-point cupping scale. Tasters
described more tropical fruit and a heavier body. The share of defective beans did not change
in a measurable way. One farm saw a vinegar taste when the tank temperature rose above 25
degrees, which shows that temperature control matters more than the length of fermentation.

## Costs

The sealed tanks added about eight percent to processing costs, mostly for the tanks
themselves and for extra labour during sealing. The higher scores sold for a premium that more
than covered this in the first season.

## Limitations

The trial covered one harvest and three farms, so the results may not hold for other altitudes
or varieties. A second season is planned with a larger group of farms and blind tasting by an
external panel.
